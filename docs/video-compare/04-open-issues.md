# Video compare feature: open issues and change history

Newest round first. All issues from rounds 1–5 have a recorded resolution.

## Round 5 critic issues and how each was resolved (revision 5)

**The check.** One critic re-ran the round-4 prototype on the revision-4 Phase 1 rules, extended to the full §3.2–3.6 pipeline: 105 synthetic cases, including adversarial ones (noise ×1–4, roll, yaw, hold + sweep, impostor dancing, squat dips).

**Results:**

| Rules | Pass |
|---|---|
| Revision 4 as written | 75/105 |
| With the two validated fixes (R71, R72) | **88/105** |

The prototype and both result files are now in `docs/video-compare/prototype/`.

**What held:**
- the "real movement" test, which rejected every negative case;
- tries;
- phases (≤ 1/20 false tips on correct takes);
- the mirror chosen from velocities;
- knee roll-in (no false claims even at 3–5× noise);
- holds, when not pause-cut;
- "no count claim" on Thattadavu-like steps.

### Issues

**[critical] pause-cut-before-kind**
- **Problem:** cutting pauses first deleted whole holds (an 89%-still teacher step became empty), frozen-aramandi students, a 3 s held pose inside a sweep, and slow movement at child noise.
- **Resolution: fixed (R71).**
  - The step kind is decided on the uncut teacher step.
  - Holds and posture steps are never cut.
  - In movement steps, only still runs whose pose matches no teacher frame are cut.
  - See 05 §3.2.

**[high] skip-charge-rejects-slow-beginners**
- **Problem:** with every skipped cell charged in full, beginners at 2–3× were rejected.
- **Resolution: fixed (R72), validated.** Slow steps charge the mean of the covered cells plus λ. Beginners are now found at 1/1.5/2/3× with spans 0.90/1.36/1.84/2.59, and negatives are still rejected (real-movement 0.94–1.00). See 05 §3.4.

**[high] m_T-is-snr**
- **Problem:** the motion ratio flips with noise; "jitter" was undefined; up/down steps are invisible.
- **Resolution: fixed (R73).** Noise-corrected motion with a threshold of 0.07 rad, stable across 1–4× noise; jitter defined once (residual of a 1 s robust fit); hip-height and segment-length features. See 05 §3.2, §3.3.

**[high] tau-collapses-near-neutral**
- **Problem:** τ_T ≈ 0.06 on Samapada-like steps; the take-median term depended on the walk-in.
- **Resolution: fixed (R74).** τ_floor and τ_P are built from the feature tolerances plus 2 × jitter cost, independent of the rest of the take. See 05 §3.4, §3.5.

**[high] pause-transitions-and-stillness-measure**
- **Problem:** standing up to pause gave false tips, and per-frame speed can't separate motion from jitter.
- **Resolution: fixed (R71).** Windowed displacement; ±1 s transitions get a neutral cost and are left unscored. See 05 §3.2.

**[high] partial-reading-undefined**
- **Problem:** getting into and out of the pose broke partial matches; range was compared with the whole step.
- **Resolution: fixed.** The reverse query is the student's frames near some teacher frame; range is compared with the matched span; the test offset is 20°. See 05 §3.4.

**[medium] noise-dependent-checks**
- **Problem:** bobbing, "part moving" and near-straight 3D angles behaved differently as noise grew.
- **Resolution: fixed (R75).** One jitter definition; noise-subtracted energy only on parts the teacher moves; 3D angle bias estimated and removed, or the feature skipped. See 05 §3.3.

**[medium] depth-tip-blocked**
- **Problem:** straight-legged students got no knee correction.
- **Resolution: fixed.** The safe "bend a little, knees over toes" tip is given when the student's legs are straight and the teacher's are bent. See 05 §3.6, 03 §5.2.

**Also fixed:**
- alignment directions are de-rolled by the median torso axis;
- bobbing is measured along the body's axis;
- tries scan past failing candidates;
- the dip test is dropped (it was never decisive in 80 cases).

**The 6 inconsistencies between 03 and 05 are all fixed:**
- the roll-in plane;
- the dip-test wording;
- the "more than 2×" wording;
- beginner depth marked Phase 2+ only;
- the R47 row marked as superseded;
- the pause exception for holds and posture steps.

**Still open, by design:** 17 synthetic cases still fail. They are mostly 3–4× noise (child-distance) and 15° roll. Fixes R73–R75 target them, and Phase 1 build step 1 has to make them pass, or write each one down as an accepted limit.

### Phase 1: algorithm (critic A)

**[critical] posture-only-steps-unalignable**
- **Problem:** Thattadavu-like steps cost about the same as their own frozen pose (motion ratio ≈ 1). As a result, correct tight and repeated takes were rejected, a frozen aramandi was "found", and speed readings were arbitrary.
- **Resolution: fixed (R57).** Each step is classified by the teacher's motion ratio. Posture steps are judged as posture plus a "part moving" check, with no timing claims (05 §3.2, §3.5).

**[critical] found-median-baseline**
- **Problem:** the median of the whole profile is the wrong baseline.
- **Resolution: fixed (R58).** The dip is measured against end costs outside the match and outside accepted tries. An absolute ceiling τ_T and a "real movement" test against the student's frozen pose were added. Validated on the non-periodic and arms-only steps (05 §3.4).

**[critical] pauses-after-alignment**
- **Problem:** pauses were handled after alignment.
- **Resolution: fixed (R59).** Pauses are cut from both videos before alignment, with an index map. Validated: 1 try, alignment error 0 (§3 F2).

**[high] partial-never-reached**
- **Resolution: fixed.** The reverse search also runs when the match is squeezed; the better per-frame reading wins; partial readings give no speed tip (05 §3.4).

**[high] slope-vs-span**
- **Resolution: fixed.** (1,3)/(3,1) steps were added; the span limits now match what the steps allow; "more than 2×" wording at the limit (05 §3.4).

**[high] median-gate-hides-phase-errors**
- **Resolution: fixed (R60).** Medians per phase (split at velocity minima), a range-of-movement check, and strengths must hold in every phase (05 §3.6, §6.1).

**[high] knee-alignment-depth-confounded**
- **Resolution: fixed (R61).** An absolute 3D roll-in on bent frames, plus the knee inside the ankle; never compared with the teacher (05 §3.3, §5.1).

**[medium] mirror-tie-rotation**
- **Resolution: fixed.** The mirror is decided on velocities; a tie uses the normal way; only tips both readings agree on are kept.

**[medium] holds-through-sdtw**
- **Resolution: fixed.** Holds bypass the step search (§3 F5, 05 §3.5).

**[medium] default-30s-range**
- **Resolution: fixed.** The default selection is the moving run (2–12 s, from pixel motion), with a warning when the selection is > 40% still (§2.1(a)).

## Round 3 critic issues (addressed in revision 3; see the resolution list below)

### alignment
- **[critical] alignment-cleaning-erases-fast-lifts: The §2.6 cleaning chain erases short ankle lifts before strike detection and makes the SNR confidence gate meaningless**
  - Scenario: A correct Adavu 1 take, recorded at 24 fps, reaches 3rd speed (IOI about 0.2 s), so each foot is in the air for about 2 samples, about 3 cm high. Or a 15 fps webcam or the 10 fps CPU fallback at 2nd speed, where a lift also lasts about 2 samples. The cleaning chain runs on these lanes before §4.1 sees them: despikeTrack(radius 2, k 3, floor 0.02 m), then stabiliseTrack(radius 3, noise 0.004·12/fps, motion 0.02·12/fps), then smoothTrack(1).
  - Why it fails: In retarget.ts, despikeTrack takes the median over 5 samples, so a lift window [0,0,3,3,0] cm has median 0 and spread 0. The limit then falls to the 0.02 m floor, and both lifted samples are replaced by 0. stabiliseTrack decides 'moving' from the median of 6 step lengths. For [0,0,0,4,4,0,0] the steps are [0,4,0,4,0,0], whose median is 0, so the joint counts as 'stationary' and is replaced by a 13-sample average (about 0.6 cm). This median-step logic was written so that 'a single bad frame cannot fake motion', and for the same reason a brief real lift cannot unlock it. The strikes vanish, and so do the ankle velocity peaks that audio needs in order to 'add' a strike (±1 sample). σ_j is measured on still frames after stabilise, which averaged away exactly that jitter, so σ_j is about 0. Then 3σ_j accepts every wiggle as a candidate, and the SNR is at least 4 on every take. The gate meant to abstain ('I couldn't count your strikes clearly') passes exactly when detection has failed, and the student gets a 0.95-importance count or 'Keep the count' tip on a correct take. The synthetic suite is built from already-cleaned bakes, so it never exercises this.
  - Suggested fix: Run the event layer (differential lift, σ_j, velocity peaks, strike times) on the raw world and screen ankle, heel and foot lanes, which carry only MediaPipe's internal filter, or on lanes cleaned with ankle-specific settings (no stabilise; despike only above an anatomically impossible jump, e.g. more than 0.25 m). Keep the current chain for the overlay and the posture features only. Measure σ_j on raw still frames. Add synthetic tests that inject 2-sample, 3 cm lifts into raw lanes at 24, 15 and 10 fps and run the full NVB2-load → clean → events path.
- **[high] alignment-fps-gate-and-systematic-misses: The fps gate uses the observed median IOI, so missed strikes inflate it and short slots are hidden; systematic detector misses then pass the recurrence rule as 'missing strike' tips**
  - Scenario: (a) In Adavu 1 at 3rd speed, the true IOI is 0.2 s, or 4.8 samples at 24 fps, so the gate should fail. The detector misses every other strike, which leaves a measured IOI of 0.4 s (9.6 samples), so the gate passes. (b) In Adavu 8 at 2nd speed the median IOI is 0.35 s (8.4 samples, passes), but the 'last three come quicker' ⅔ slots are 0.23 s, have smaller lifts, and are missed in most phrases. In Adavu 5 the 'three quick ones' behave the same way. (c) A beginner stamps the right foot at 8 cm and the left at 2 cm. The shared per-section threshold of 0.4 × median lift (about 2 cm) drops most left strikes.
  - Why it fails: The gate is computed from the detector's own output, and a median ignores the shortest slots, which are the ones at risk. The misses in (b) and (c) are not random: they fall at the same reference positions every phrase. They therefore satisfy 'same edit at same reference position in ≥50% of phrases', and the app confidently says 'you missed one of the last three strikes' or flags a same/switch error. A detector bias is reported as the student's error. Strike confidence is also computed per take, so strong 1st-speed lifts certify the 3rd-speed section.
  - Suggested fix: Gate on the expected IOI, which is slotDur × τ with τ estimated from the long slots or from neighbouring sections × the ladder ratio. Apply it per slot class (the shortest slot), not to the section median. Compute strike confidence per section, and per foot (lift SNR of each foot separately), with per-foot thresholds. Do not report a deletion edit at a reference position whose expected IOI is under 5 samples, or whose matched lifts on other phrases are near threshold, unless stamps confirm it. Add calibration cases: Adavu 8 and Adavu 5 correct at 2nd speed, and an asymmetric-strength correct take, with no count tip expected.
- **[high] alignment-phrase-correctness-ignores-detection-error: The 'Keep the count' rule (under 50% exact phrases) fires on correct students because per-strike detection errors compound over long phrases and short takes**
  - Scenario: Adavu 7 or 8 has 14 strikes per phrase. If the detector achieves 95% per-strike precision and recall, which is good for pose at home, a perfectly danced phrase aligns exactly with probability 0.95^14 ≈ 0.49 (0.97^14 ≈ 0.65). With the default auto-stop of 3 phrases, a partial first or last phrase plus one random miss gives 1/3 exact.
  - Why it fails: Phrase correctness has a fixed threshold (50%) and is triggered by an exact-match count. It does not account for the expected detection error rate, the phrase length, partial boundary phrases or the number of phrases. Its preconditions, 'detection is confident and no single edit recurs', describe exactly what random detector noise looks like. The result is an importance-0.95 'Keep the count. 2 of 6 phrases had the right strikes' given to a dancer who made no errors. It is the second-ranked tip in the system and is not one of the 6 calibrated v1 templates.
  - Suggested fix: Base the decision on the per-strike edit rate, compared with a per-fps-tier detector error rate measured in calibration on good takes (for example, a one-sided binomial test that the student's edit rate is at least 3× the detector rate, with at least 4 complete phrases). Exclude partial first and last phrases. Report the ✓/✗ strip only for phrases whose edits are stamp-confirmed or high-SNR. Make 'Keep the count' its own calibrated template, with good long-phrase takes required to give no tip.
- **[critical] alignment-systematic-error-labelled-other-step: F3b run labelling turns the most common Thattadavu error, dancing a neighbouring adavu's pattern, into 'other step: not judged', so the flagship count tip can never fire**
  - Scenario: A student declares Adavu 3 and strikes R R · L L throughout. This is §6.4 tip #3, 'you struck twice on each side'. Her string matches the Adavu 2 card at zero cost. A student doing Adavu 7 who forgets the L on count four and rests instead produces exactly Adavu 6 (R R R _ R R R _). A student doing Adavu 8 who stamps on the silent eight produces almost pure alternation, which matches the Adavu 1 card (R L) with foot cost 0 over the whole phrase.
  - Why it fails: F3b labels each run with the best-matching card and judges only runs matching the declared step. The adavus are a graded family that differ by one count or one rest, so a student's systematic error equals another card exactly, and that card wins every 2-phrase window. The run is shown as 'other step: not judged', and the pattern tip, the core v1 deliverable, is suppressed. The design's own test (Adavu 1–4 concatenated, declared Adavu 3) cannot be told apart from a student who believes she is doing Adavu 3. Window costs are also compared across candidates with very different window lengths (2 phrases of Adavu 1 = 4 strikes; of Adavu 7 = 28 strikes), and no normalisation is specified. Short cards such as Adavu 1 will therefore win locally inside longer steps (Adavu 8's R L R L prefix), which fragments correct takes.
  - Suggested fix: Give the declared step a strong prior. Label a run as another step only when (1) it is bounded within the same take by runs that match the declared step, or the user confirms it, and (2) the declared step's per-strike normalised cost exceeds a fixed edit rate (for example 0.25 per strike) while the other card is near 0. When the whole take matches a different card, ask: 'You danced Adavu 2's pattern (2 per side). Were you practising Adavu 3?' If she answers yes, report it as a count error. Normalise window cost per aligned strike, and use windows of equal duration rather than equal phrase count. Add tests: declared Adavu 3 with 2 per side throughout gives the count tip; Adavu 7 missing the switch gives a pattern tip; Adavu 8 with a stamp on eight gives the silence tip, not 'other step'.
- **[high] alignment-version-check-swallows-consistent-errors: A consistent wrong pattern is classed as 'a different version' even after the student confirmed the version**
  - Scenario: The student answers 'Yes, this is how I learn it' to the version check for Adavu 4 (R R R R · L L L L). She then dances R R R · L L L in every phrase, a classic error, at 90% self-repeat.
  - Why it fails: The rule 'self-consistent (≥85%) but differs from P in every phrase → different version: pattern not judged' cannot tell a bani difference from a learned mistake. Beginners who have practised a wrong count are the most self-consistent students. The student has already said this is her version, and the app overrides that and says nothing. The tip example '5 of 6 phrases' only works if at least one phrase is right, so the consistent errors that most need correcting get no feedback.
  - Suggested fix: If the student confirmed the version, report consistent deviations as pattern errors, worded relative to the reference ('this reference strikes four times on each side; you struck three every time'), with a one-tap 'My version is different' that switches pattern tips off for the project. Infer 'different version' automatically only when the student has not confirmed, and even then show the detected strip and ask instead of silently not judging.
- **[high] alignment-start-foot-absorbed-by-free-start: The free-start cyclic DP absorbs a wrong start foot as a zero-cost rotation, so the start-foot rule can never fire, and the fix would collide with the 'start at count 4' test**
  - Scenario: Laterality is known. A student dances Adavu 3 correctly in shape but starts with the left foot: L L L · R R R …
  - Why it fails: The alignment has a free start over j mod |P|, so it matches her first strike to reference position 4 (the L half-phrase), with foot agreement, at cost 0. §4.2 gives a start-foot tip only when the first aligned strike 'maps to position 1 … with the opposite foot', and a min-cost DP never produces that alignment when a cost-0 rotation exists. Every Thattadavu card is an R-half plus a mirrored L-half, so the rule is dead on all of them. Rewriting it as 'first strike maps to the L half-phrase start' does not help either: on a symmetric phrase that string is identical to a correct dancer who started late at count 4, which the synthetic suite says must give no tip. Start foot is one of the 6 v1 calibration templates, so 40 recorded instances would fail the gate.
  - Suggested fix: Anchor the start in time, not only in the string. For recorder takes, the count-in defines where position 1 is expected (the first strike within about 1 pulse after the count-in). A wrong start foot is then 'first strike at the expected time, aligned to the opposite half-phrase start'. For uploads without an anchor beat, abstain from start-foot claims (or ask 'Did you start on count 1?'). Add a unit test: L-start at the count-in gives the tip, and a late start at count 4 gives no tip.
- **[medium] alignment-rest-slots-undefined-in-dp: Rest slots and the timing term are under-specified in the cyclic DP, so 'no pause' is invisible and inserted strikes have no fixed position**
  - Scenario: Adavu 6 (R R R _ R R R _; cue 'A pause after each group'): the student runs straight through the rests (R R R R R R). In Adavu 7, the student stamps on the silent eight.
  - Why it fails: The timing term is 0.5·|log(IOI_i/(slotDur_j·τ))| with the matched slot's duration only. The design does not say whether rest slots are DP positions (whose deletion would charge every correct dancer 1 per rest) or are skipped for free. If they are skipped for free and the IOI is not compared with the sum of slot durations between consecutive matched positions, a collapsed rest costs 0.5·ln2 ≈ 0.35. That is below any edit, so it is never reported, and it later shows up as a residual-IOI outlier: either a misworded 'uneven strikes' tip or nothing. An inserted strike has no timing term at all, so whether it is 'a strike on eight' or 'an extra strike somewhere' depends on tie-breaking. That makes the silent-slot template unstable across phrases and weakens the 'same position' recurrence test.
  - Suggested fix: Define rests as zero-cost skippable positions. Compute the timing term on each IOI against the summed slot durations between the two matched positions, including deleted and rest slots. Treat a strike that falls inside a rest slot's time window as a 'strike on silent slot' edit, with timing-based placement. Add a 'rest collapsed' edit (an IOI across a rest under 0.6× expected, in at least 50% of phrases) with its own tip and calibration case, and exclude rest-spanning IOIs from evenness.
- **[medium] alignment-ladder-ramp-staircase: Speed sectioning stair-steps a rushing student, and ladder pairing then gives a false 'make 2nd speed twice as fast'**
  - Scenario: Adavu 1. The student rushes 1st speed, with the pulse shortening 30% over 16 strikes, then correctly doubles. Binary segmentation (at least 4 strikes per section) fits the ramp as two plateaus, 1.0 and 1.3. The global BIC choice is between all-piecewise-constant and all-linear, and a real ladder plus drift is not linear, so piecewise-constant wins. ## Round 3: how each issue was resolved (revision 3)

Status words: **fixed** (the design now does it), **moved** (the feature it applied to is now in a later phase, where it is fixed), **avoided** (Phase 1 no longer has the mechanism), **accepted** (listed in 03-design.md §13). Section numbers refer to `03-design.md` (revision 3) and `05-mvp.md`.

### Critical
- alignment-cleaning-erases-fast-lifts → **fixed**: events run on raw lanes (MediaPipe's own filter only; despike only jumps > 0.25 m); σ_j is measured on raw still frames; the mocap chain is for the overlay only; raw-lane injection tests at 24/15/10 fps. See §2.6, §4.1 and R42. Phase 1 uses no mocap filters for features.
- alignment-systematic-error-labelled-other-step → **fixed**: the declared step has a strong prior; a single-pattern take is never relabelled; splitting happens only at pauses or change points in multi-pattern takes; costs are normalised per strike over equal durations; tests. See §3 F3b and R43.
- feedback-consistent-count-error-swallowed → **fixed**: neighbouring cards are likely errors, so a whole-take match to another card asks "Which were you practising?"; "different version" applies only when no card matches and the version is unconfirmed. See §3 F3b and §4.2.
- completeness-consistent-count-error-relabelled-as-other-step-or-version → **fixed**: the version "Yes" is authoritative; consistent deviation is an error with a "My version is different" button; tests for declared Adavu 3 as R R · L L, Adavu 7 missing the switch, and Adavu 8 stamping on 8. See §4.2 and §9.
- legal-ux-user-scope-is-one-shared-demo-id → **fixed**: student video is never kept. Phase 1 holds it in memory only. Phase 2's crash-resume record is AES-encrypted with a key in sessionStorage and swept from the root layout on every page load. "Keep" is removed, and summaries go in a separate key. See §2.7 and R50.

### High

## How rounds 1-2 issues were resolved

### Round 1
- alignment-absolute-offset-swamps-ratio-tests → **fixed**: §4.0 separates the two feature sets. Alignment uses per-take de-meaned features with fixed scaling plus velocity. Confidence uses a 0.5 s block-shuffled null and a best-vs-second-best test, so the absolute τ is gone. Absolute differences are measured only after alignment. A synthetic case with a 20° offset must stay aligned.
- alignment-viterbi-integer-phase-advance → **fixed**: The phase Viterbi is removed. Footwork uses piecewise-linear warping between matched strikes (§4.4), and other steps use chunked sDTW (§4.5). Synthetic tests cover 0.35, 2, 8 and 16 s phrases.
- alignment-sdtw-step-weights-skip-query → **fixed**: In §4.5 the (2,1) step now charges the skipped query cell, so every query frame is paid once. Unit tests cover dropped frames (same cost) and a missing hold (higher cost).
- alignment-long-cycles-exceed-acf → **fixed**: The phrase period is the smallest exact repeat of the strike string over {R,L,_} / {S,X,_} (§4.1), so Adavu 7 gives 16 slots. The ACF fallback uses lags 0.2 s to min(20 s, step/2) over the whole step, with windows at least 2.5× the period.
- alignment-novelty-splits-at-side-switch → **fixed**: Novelty is computed on mirror-invariant features (L+R and |L−R|), and neighbouring segments are merged when segment k+1 matches mirror(segment k) (§3 F3). Synthetic test: an R-block then L-block must not split.
- alignment-absolute-tempo-without-beat → **fixed**: Speed labels are relative to the student's own ladder, with contiguous geometric bands (√2, 2√2) and an 'uncertain' margin. A single-section take prompts 'Which speed?'. Rushing and dragging are measured as drift within the take, and early/late only with the recorder clock (§4.1, §5.3).
